> **On the name.** An *egregore* is the thought-form that arises from a group's shared attention — an entity generated by, and belonging to, the collective rather than any individual in it. That is precisely what this system makes visible. Working title; alternates include *Oneiron*, *The Long Dream*, and *Somnium*.

---

## 1. Summary

Egregore listens to the conversations happening across a physical space, distills them into abstract themes, and renders those themes as continuously evolving, symbolic, mesmerizing video on screens throughout the room. Over the course of a night, the imagery accumulates into a growing loop — a visual record of everything the room has been thinking about.

Speech never leaves the building. Transcription and theme-abstraction run on local hardware; only an abstracted, non-attributable aesthetic prompt is ever sent to an external service, and only when the operator has chosen a cloud generation backend at all.

The system is designed as a **configurable instrument**, not a fixed installation: a party operator defines listening zones, an aesthetic grammar, a spend budget, and a set of screens, and the system runs itself for the night.

---

## 2. The experience

You walk into a space. There are screens — projected on a wall, propped in a corner, mounted above the bar. They are showing something that is not quite anything: forms that suggest architecture and then suggest anatomy, colors that bloom and drift, geometries folding through themselves. It is beautiful and it is slow and it holds your eye longer than you expected.

You stand near the screen in the east corner for a while and start to notice: the imagery here is different from the imagery by the kitchen. Somebody says the thing about the ocean, about how her grandmother described it, and twenty minutes later the corner is full of vast blue depths that nobody can quite account for.

The image is breathing. Not metaphorically — the whole frame pulses and warps with the sound in the room, tightening when the music drops, blooming outward when the room laughs. It responds at the speed of the moment even though the imagery itself changes on the scale of minutes.

Nothing ever cuts. The dream never restarts. It goes on all night and it is *longer* at 2am than it was at 10pm, because everything it has already dreamed is still in there, cycling.

Nobody's words are on the screen. Nobody is quoted. Nobody is identifiable. What is on the screen is what the room has been *about*.

## 3. The central design tension

**Generative video is slow and expensive. Parties are fast and long.**

A cloud video generation runs on the order of tens of seconds to minutes per clip. Local generation on available hardware can run to many minutes per clip. Cloud generation costs real money per second of output, which multiplies hard across many zones over many hours. A naive "generate a video every time someone says something interesting" design would be either bankrupting, badly laggy, or both.

The resolution is to stop treating this as one system running at one speed. **Egregore is three temporal layers stacked on top of each other**, and the felt sense of a living, responsive room comes almost entirely from the fastest one:

| Layer | Cadence | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shader layer** | 60 fps | Warps, colors, feeds back, pulses. Bound to live audio from the room. *This is what makes the space feel alive and responsive.* | Free (client GPU) |
| **Mood layer** | ~1–10 s | Rolling audio features and drifting theme vector. Modulates shader parameters and biases the next prompt. | Free (local) |
| **Imagery layer** | ~1–10 min | New generated video enters the loop. *This is what makes the space feel meaningful.* | Metered — cloud $ or local GPU time |

Getting this stratification right is the single most important product decision in the document. It means the system is honest about generative latency instead of fighting it, and it means the budget knob controls the slow layer without ever making the room feel dead.

**What this means quantitatively, stated plainly.** At a realistic budget, freshly generated video covers roughly 5–15% of total screen-time. The other 85–95% is previously generated material recycled through the growing loop under live shader modulation. This is not a compromise to be engineered away — it is the design. A viewer perceives continuity and responsiveness from the shader layer and *meaning* from the imagery layer, and the imagery layer does not need to be novel every second to deliver that. Any claim in this document about "continuous" or "unbroken" imagery should be read against this ratio.

## 4. Users and contexts

### 4.1 Primary user — the Operator

The person running the night. Sets up the kit, defines zones, writes or picks the aesthetic grammar, sets the budget, and then wants to *stop thinking about it* and go be at the party. Technical enough to edit a config file and run a command; not willing to babysit a terminal for six hours.

**Needs:** fast setup, sane defaults, a glanceable status view, graceful failure, a hard spend ceiling they trust.

### 4.2 Secondary user — the Guest

Doesn't know how it works and shouldn't need to. Experiences it as environment, not interface. May be in an altered state.

**Needs:** nothing jarring, nothing that reads as surveillance, nothing that breaks the spell — no error dialogs, no loading spinners, no black screens, no sudden brightness changes.

### 4.3 Contexts

- **House party / salon** — 1–2 zones, 2–3 screens, 4–6 hours, modest budget.
- **Ritual / ceremonial container** — 2–4 zones, careful aesthetic direction, high sensitivity to tone; imagery must never become frightening or destabilizing. Overlaps with the Herd of God / Six Wall Productions body of work.
- **Large event / warehouse** — 4–8 zones, many screens, long duration, remote screens joining over the internet.
- **Durational installation** — runs for days on the local backend at zero marginal cost, accumulating an enormous loop.

## 5. Product principles

1. **The room's speech is sacred.** Raw audio and raw text are ephemeral, local, and never persisted. The privacy architecture is a feature of the artwork, not a compliance checkbox.
2. **Abstraction over illustration.** The system renders what a conversation *feels* and *means*, never what it literally says. No one should ever be able to look at a screen and identify who said what.
3. **The dream never breaks.** No cuts to black, no restarts, no visible loading. Every transition is a dissolve. Degradation is always graceful and always invisible to guests.
4. **Continuity is the aesthetic.** One long evolving dream beats a shuffle of disconnected clips.
5. **The instrument is tunable.** Aesthetic grammar, pacing, budget, and shader character are all configuration, not code.
6. **Zero-cost must be a real option.** A fully local pipeline that costs nothing and touches no external service is a first-class supported mode, not a degraded fallback.
7. **Beauty over novelty.** Conceptual, symbolic, deep, mesmerizing. Not glitchy-for-its-own-sake, not meme-y, not literal.

## 6. Requirements

Priority: **P0** = required for a first real party · **P1** = required for the product to be good · **P2** = future.

### 6.1 Listening and zones

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| L-1 | P0 | Support N independently configured listening zones, each with a name, a microphone source, and an associated set of screens. |
| L-2 | P0 | Continuously capture audio per zone with voice activity detection, so silence and pure music do not generate spurious text. |
| L-3 | P0 | Transcribe locally, on hardware under the operator's control. No audio or transcript may be sent to any external service. |
| L-4 | P0 | Maintain only a short rolling in-memory window of recent text per zone (default 5 minutes). Never write transcripts to disk. |
| L-5 | P1 | Handle a realistic party acoustic environment — loud music, overlapping speakers, laughter — without producing hallucinated text during non-speech audio. |
| L-6 | P0 | Extract live audio features (RMS, spectral bands, onset) per zone at high rate, independent of transcription, for the shader layer. *(P0 because VIS-2 depends on it.)* |
| L-7 | P1 | Zone-to-zone "bleed," where a strong theme in one zone can influence a neighbouring zone's imagery. *(P1 rather than P2: the degradation ladder routes a dead microphone to neighbouring zones, so this path must exist for that failure mode to work.)* |

### 6.2 Theme extraction and prompt synthesis

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| T-1 | P0 | Convert a rolling window of zone text into an abstract **theme object** — motifs, emotional tone, conceptual register — using a locally hosted language model. |
| T-2 | P0 | Minimize the risk that the theme object contains names, quotes, verbatim phrases, or personally identifying content, through a layered defense: instruction, a deterministic validator, and a data-flow boundary that keeps raw text out of the prompt-synthesis stage. See Architecture §2.4 for what each layer does and does not guarantee — the abstraction step itself is model-mediated and cannot be called structurally guaranteed. |
| T-3 | P0 | Synthesize a video generation prompt from the theme object plus the party's configured **aesthetic grammar**. |
| T-4 | P0 | Make the aesthetic grammar (the system prompt governing visual style, symbolic register, and mood) fully configurable per party. |
| T-5 | P1 | Maintain thematic memory across the night, so imagery evolves and returns to earlier motifs rather than resetting each generation. |
| T-6 | P1 | Bias prompt synthesis toward continuity with the currently playing imagery when continuity mode is active. |
| T-7 | P1 | Support a "drift" parameter controlling how tightly imagery tracks conversation versus wandering associatively. |
| T-8 | P2 | Use a purpose-tuned local model for prompt synthesis (candidate: the Golden Seed AI corpus model) rather than a general instruct model. |

### 6.3 Video generation

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| V-1 | P0 | Generate video from a synthesized prompt through a pluggable backend interface. |
| V-2 | P0 | Support a cloud backend (Google Veo family) with selectable model tier. |
| V-3 | P0 | Generate **without audio** by default — the room supplies its own sound, and video-only generation is 33–50% cheaper depending on tier. |
| V-4 | P0 | Support a fully local backend running open-weights video models on operator hardware at zero marginal cost. *(P0 because principle 6 and B-6 both depend on it.)* |
| V-5 | P1 | Fail over between backends automatically — e.g. exhausted budget or a cloud outage transparently switches to local. |
| V-6 | P1 | Maintain a generation queue that stays one or more clips ahead per zone, so new imagery is always ready before it is needed. |
| V-7 | P2 | Support reference images to pin a party's visual identity across all generations. |

### 6.4 Continuity and the growing loop

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| C-1 | P0 | **Mosaic mode:** accumulate generated clips into a growing playlist that cycles continuously, with dissolve transitions. The loop lengthens through the night. |
| C-2 | P1 | **Continuity mode:** chain generations so each new segment visually continues from the last frame of the previous one, producing long unbroken *movements*. Note that at realistic budgets a movement is replayed far more than it is extended — see §3 on what "continuous" honestly means here. |
| C-3 | P1 | Continuity must survive past the cloud provider's per-chain extension ceiling by seeding a new chain from the previous chain's final frame. The handoff must preserve both composition and **direction of motion** — a sudden change of drift direction is the seam. |
| C-4 | P1 | Continuity mode is a toggle, switchable per zone, changeable mid-party without restart. |
| C-5 | P0 | The loop must never expose a gap: while nothing new has been generated, existing material continues cycling under live shader modulation. |
| C-6 | P1 | Weight the loop toward recency — newer clips appear more often than older ones — while never fully retiring older material. |
| C-7 | P1 | Persist the night's full sequence as a retrievable artifact: the party's dream, exportable after the fact. |

### 6.5 Real-time visual layer

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| VIS-1 | P0 | Composite a configurable stack of GLSL shader effects over the playing video, rendering at display framerate. |
| VIS-2 | P0 | Drive shader parameters from live audio features so the visuals visibly respond to the room. |
| VIS-3 | P0 | Crossfade between clips at the shader level — never a hard cut. |
| VIS-4 | P1 | Ship a library of named shader "lenses" (feedback, kaleidoscope, flow-warp, chromatic drift, bloom, liquid) composable into stacks and selectable per party or per screen. |
| VIS-5 | P1 | Allow each screen to run a different lens stack and a different phase offset in the loop, so no two screens are ever identical. |
| VIS-6 | P1 | Allow a screen to react to its own local microphone instead of its zone's feed — so a remote screen responds to its own room. |
| VIS-7 | P0 | Degrade gracefully on weak client hardware by reducing shader complexity rather than dropping frames. |

> **ID convention.** Visual-layer requirements use `VIS-*` to avoid collision with the Phase 0 spike IDs (`S-1`…`S-7`) in the Implementation Plan, which mean something entirely different.

### 6.6 Delivery and screens

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | P0 | A screen is any device with a browser. Joining is: open a URL, enter a password, go fullscreen. |
| D-2 | P0 | Serve to screens on the local network with no internet dependency. |
| D-3 | P1 | Serve to screens beyond the local network over the public internet, password-protected. |
| D-4 | P0 | Screens self-heal — a device that sleeps, drops connection, or reloads rejoins the dream without operator intervention. |
| D-5 | P1 | Screens can be assigned to a zone by URL parameter, so setup is per-device and stateless. |
| D-6 | P2 | Operator can push an aesthetic change or a "moment" (e.g. a synchronized shift across all screens) live. |

### 6.7 Budget governor

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| B-1 | P0 | Accept a **total spend budget** and a **party duration**, and derive a generation cadence that spends the budget evenly across the night. |
| B-2 | P0 | Enforce a hard ceiling equal to the configured budget. The system must be structurally incapable of exceeding it, and this must not depend on the accuracy of its own cost estimates. |
| B-3 | P1 | Support a spend curve so cadence can accelerate during peak hours and slow during arrival and wind-down. |
| B-4 | P1 | On budget exhaustion, fail over to the local backend rather than stopping — the dream continues, just free. |
| B-5 | P1 | Report live spend, projected spend, and remaining budget to the operator. |
| B-6 | P0 | Support a zero-budget configuration: local backend only, no cloud calls possible. |

### 6.8 Privacy

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| P-1 | P0 | Audio is processed in memory and discarded. Never written to disk, never transmitted outside the local network. |
| P-2 | P0 | Transcribed text lives only in a bounded in-memory ring buffer and is never written to disk or logged. |
| P-3 | P0 | Only the abstracted prompt may cross the network boundary to a cloud service, and only in cloud backend mode. |
| P-4 | P0 | A fully local mode exists in which no data of any kind leaves the operator's hardware. |
| P-5 | P0 | Ship physical signage copy and a verbal framing script for the operator to disclose the system's presence to guests. The copy must be **literally true in the mode being run** — in cloud mode it must disclose that an abstracted prompt is sent to an external service. |
| P-6 | P1 | Provide a visible, physical mute per zone — guests can silence a listening point themselves. |
| P-7 | P1 | Make the privacy posture inspectable: a status view showing what is retained (nothing) and what has been sent (prompt count only). |

### 6.9 Configuration

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| G-1 | P0 | A single declarative config file defines an entire party: zones, screens, aesthetic grammar, budget, backends, shader stacks, continuity mode. |
| G-2 | P0 | Ship preset configurations for the common contexts in §4.3. |
| G-3 | P1 | Hot-reload config changes mid-party without dropping the dream. |
| G-4 | P1 | An operator dashboard showing per-zone status, queue depth, spend, and screen count. |
| G-5 | P2 | Config presets shareable as portable files, so aesthetics can be authored by one person and run by another. |

## 7. Success criteria

**The night worked if:**

1. No guest ever saw a black screen, a loading state, an error, or a hard cut.
2. At least one guest spontaneously noticed that the imagery related to something being discussed nearby — without being told the system existed.
3. The operator did not touch the system between starting it and shutting it down.
4. Final spend landed within 10% *under* the configured budget and never exceeded it. (The band is deliberately one-sided: overspend is a failure, underspend is only a missed opportunity.)
5. The imagery was, on the operator's own aesthetic judgment, genuinely beautiful for the majority of the night — not merely functional.
6. Nothing was retained afterward except the generated video sequence itself.

**The product worked if:**

7. A second operator can run a party from a preset config without reading the source.
8. Setup time from arriving at a venue to first imagery on screen is under 30 minutes.

## 8. Non-goals

- **Not a VJ tool.** No live human performance interface, no beat-matching, no MIDI control surface. The room is the performer.
- **Not a transcription or analytics product.** The system produces no record of what anyone said, by design. There is no "what did the party talk about" report.
- **Not real-time video generation.** Imagery arrives on a scale of minutes. Anyone expecting instant response to a spoken sentence has misunderstood the instrument.
- **Not literal illustration.** The system will never render a recognizable depiction of the topic under discussion.
- **Not identity-aware.** No speaker diarization, no voice fingerprinting, no per-person anything. Zones are places, not people.
- **Not a content-moderation system.** Aesthetic guardrails live in the prompt grammar; this is not a safety classifier for what guests discuss.

---

## 9. Open questions

1. **Aesthetic seeding.** Should a party's visual identity be pinned by reference images supplied up front, or emerge purely from the grammar text? Reference images give far more consistency but constrain surprise.
2. **Zone distinctness.** How different *should* zones look? Fully independent dreams, or variations on one shared aesthetic with local inflection? Current lean: shared grammar, independent theme streams.
3. **The frightening-imagery problem.** In a ceremonial or psychedelic context, an unsettling image on a screen is not a neutral event. Do we need a hard aesthetic floor — a set of prompt constraints that structurally prevent horror, faces in distress, threatening forms — enforced independent of the party grammar? Current lean: **yes**, as a non-overridable base layer.
4. **The retention question.** C-7 wants the night's dream exportable, which is in tension with the privacy posture. The video is abstract and non-attributable, so this is probably fine — but it should be an explicit operator opt-in rather than a default.
5. **Model choice for prompt synthesis.** A general instruct model is the fast path. The Golden Seed AI model would produce a distinctly different symbolic register and is thematically apt. Worth an experiment once Golden Seed has a usable checkpoint.
6. **Naming.** Egregore is a strong concept fit but carries occult connotation that may or may not be desirable depending on the deployment context.
